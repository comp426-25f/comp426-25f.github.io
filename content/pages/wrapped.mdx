## Content + Connections Beyond 426

In total, COMP 426 held **25** total content lectures throughout the semester across **six core modules** - <span className="font-semibold bg-slate-200 dark:bg-[#2c3744] text-slate-900 dark:text-slate-100 px-2 py-0.5 rounded">M01: Fundamentals</span>, <span className="font-semibold bg-[#c9daf8] dark:bg-[#1e3a5f] text-blue-950 dark:text-blue-100 px-2 py-0.5 rounded">M02: React</span>, <span className="font-semibold bg-[#DFDCEC] dark:bg-[#352c47] text-purple-950 dark:text-purple-100 px-2 py-0.5 rounded">M03: Web App Architectures</span>, <span className="font-semibold bg-[#DEECDC] dark:bg-[#1a3d2e] text-emerald-950 dark:text-emerald-100 px-2 py-0.5 rounded">M04: Backend</span>, <span className="font-semibold bg-[#F1E1E9] dark:bg-[#4a2a3d] text-pink-950 dark:text-pink-100 px-2 py-0.5 rounded">M05: Design</span>, and <span className="font-semibold bg-[#fff2cc] dark:bg-[#4a3d1a] text-amber-950 dark:text-amber-100 px-2 py-0.5 rounded">M06: Technologies</span>. This course was originally designed with five core goals in mind:

1. Understand the _architecture_ and _workflow_ for building _production-grade_ full-stack web applications (concepts applicable beyond any specific framework!)
2. Gain hands-on experience with both _frontend_ and _backend_ development concepts.
3. Appreciate and have practice with implementing _good design practices_.
4. Build skills reading documentation and _habitualizing self-learning_.
5. Prepare students for internships or industry careers in full-stack development.

Throughout the course, students built up their full-stack web development toolkit with modern tools and frameworks informed by industry best practices and standards. However, this course was designed **not** to just teach the tools of the day. In the web development world, industry preferences for tools move *fast!* Many of the tools used in this course are less than a decade old. In another decade, many of the tools used in this course will be obsolete. Lectures were intentionally designed to teach *core foundational principles* at the heart of web programming and to understand ***why*** we choose to use the tools we do. With this in mind, students will be empowered to learn and adapt to new tools and frameworks as they emerge.

In addition, while this course focuses on software engineering through the lens of web development, most of the content covered in COMP 426 **builds upon principles rooted at the core of computer science**, with deep connections to many foundational courses across our curriculum. Highlighting these connections was an intentional design decision for the course. By being exposed to these topics grounded in real-world applications, students are encouraged to dive deeper into these topics and build connections between different courses and topics across our curriculum to not only strengthen their understanding, but to appreciate how so much of the software engineering we do today builds upon the work of decades of research and innovation in the field of computer science.

Students were also graded on other technical best-practices, including:
* Ensuring accessibility and SEO best practices are followed. All students were required to run the Lighthouse Report and ensure that the scores were ≥90 for both the accessibility and SEO sections.

* Using ESLint / Prettier to enforce code style and consistency.
